# Frontier AI Exploration Plan

> **Paused 2026-11-03.** Phase 1 complete; Phase 2 has SimCLR, SimSiam and VICReg done (Steps 2–5). Remaining: Phase 2 Steps 6–8, Phases 3–4. Active work moved to [Epic 2](epic2_plan_03-11-2026.md).

## Focus

Two complementary themes at the core of this exploration:

- **Representation Learning (RL):** What structure *should* a model learn?
- **Mechanistic Interpretability (MI):** What structure *did* the model learn?

## Constraints

- ~20 hours/week
- Single RTX 4090
- Open-source models and tooling only

**Goal:** Learn deeply and start contributing — not build products.

---

## The Phases

### Phase 1 — Learn a Representation

> Detailed breakdown: [`phase1_plan_23-07-2026.md`](epic1_phase1_plan_23-07-2026.md)

**Study**
-
- Latent spaces

**Build**
- Train a tiny VAE on:
  - MNIST
  - Fashion-MNIST
  - CIFAR-10 (optional)
- Sweep the latent dimension: 2 → 8 → 32 → 128
- Visualize:
  - Latent clusters
  - Interpolations
  - Reconstructions

**Questions to answer**
- What information gets preserved?
- What gets discarded?
- What makes a representation useful?

> Building this creates intuition that papers alone rarely provide.

### Phase 2 — Self-Supervised Learning

> Detailed breakdown: [`epic1_phase2_plan_26-07-2026.md`](epic1_phase2_plan_26-07-2026.md)

**Study**
- SimCLR
- BYOL
- VICReg
- DINO

**Build**

- Dataset: CIFAR-10 or STL-10
- Extract embeddings → run t-SNE or UMAP → visualize clusters

**Experiment**
- Strong vs. weak augmentations
- Observe:
  - Which invariances emerge?
  - When does representation quality degrade?

> Gives a concrete understanding of what the objective is actually doing.

### Phase 3 — Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)

This is where things become directly relevant to frontier MI.

**Build**
- Take a small open model:
  - Gemma 2B
  - Pythia 410M
  - TinyLlama
- Capture activations from:
  - An MLP layer
  - The residual stream
- Train a tiny SAE

**Questions to answer**
- What features emerge?
- Are they interpretable?
- How sparse should the dictionary be?

> Very close to current interpretability workflows. A 4090 is sufficient for meaningful experiments here.

---

### Phase 4 — Mechanistic Interpretability

**Build**
- Use:
  - TransformerLens
  - Neuronpedia
- Replicate a known result:
  - Induction heads
  - IOI (Indirect Object Identification)
  - Simple factual recall circuits

**Goal**
- Learn the tooling
- Learn activation patching
- Learn causal tracing

> Don't invent new science yet. This is the equivalent of reproducing classic experiments in a physics lab.

## Stretch: A Longitudinal Project

**"From Representation to Features"** — one project spanning all four phases, optimizing for an eventual transition into research.

1. Train a small representation model
2. Extract latent activations
3. Train an SAE on those activations
4. Analyze discovered features
5. Compare features across training checkpoints

**Research question:** *How do interpretable features emerge during training?*

This sits exactly at the intersection of representation learning and mechanistic interpretability. It is:

- Computationally feasible on a 4090
- Scientifically interesting
- Not saturated with thousands of researchers
- Close to current frontier work from organizations such as Anthropic and EleutherAI

> Of all possible additions, this longitudinal "feature emergence" project has the highest chance of turning passive learning into genuine research intuition.
